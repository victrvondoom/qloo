## What each one teaches

1. **Arcon is the closest and the broadest.** Floor plans, 3D, budgets, procurement and a builder workspace are a different product size. Its verification is schema and layout validity plus rule-based edits; the page does not describe a per-requirement verification report, a semantic diff of a change request, or a leave-one-reference-out stability analysis. We should not try to match its breadth.
2. **Tastecraft** shows how to be believed: fix the metric and the decision rule before looking, report an interval that includes no-effect as "not proven", and say the ruler is Qloo's own tags. Our benchmark's oracle shares our rulebook; we say so, but we have no held-out, pre-registered evaluation yet.
3. **FanFit and Simpatico** validate *claims about evidence* (cited numbers must match the ledger). Our prose is already grounded against a fact sheet; our specification lines cite ledger entries but nothing re-checks them independently of the agent that wrote them.
4. **Tasteplot** reinforces baseline-adjusted comparison, which we already do per atmosphere (crowd share over city share).
5. **Simpatico's ablations and TasteTest's leave-one-signal-out** show that removing a signal is a convincing robustness check. We have a leave-one-out influence map but present it as attribution, not as a stability verdict on the specification.
6. **Rekindle** is the model for honesty about the API: typed PASS/REFUSE/REVIEW verdicts, a human gate, and a written list of unsafe assumptions.

## Gaps this comparison exposes in Tastespec

- Not live-verified and not deployed (competitors above state live demos). Blocked on credentials, not engineering.
- No professional, versioned, exportable specification document (Tasteplot has a memo; Arcon has a drawings pack).
- A change request is a full re-run with no record of which lines changed *meaningfully* and why (Arcon re-checks edits; ours does not diff at all).
- Specification lines are not independently re-verified against constraints, evidence and rules in typed, per-line diagnostics (Rekindle's verifier is the standard to meet).
- No pre-registered or held-out evaluation.
- No recorded-live replay mode for after the hackathon key expires (FanFit, Simpatico).

## Where Tastespec can credibly be different

A narrow professional capability rather than a wider product: **compile** client language and cultural references into a versioned specification; **verify** every line against hard limits, evidence and rules with typed diagnostics; **revise** with a semantic diff that says what changed, what was preserved, and what must be re-checked; **stress** it by removing one reference at a time; **export** a review-ready document that states its unknowns. None of the pages above describe that combined loop for lighting and finishes specifications. That is an observation about their READMEs, not a claim that they lack it or a measured advantage.
